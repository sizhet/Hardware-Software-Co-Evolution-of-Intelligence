**Optimization / Placement Figure**

## Core Structure

The figure organizes intelligence around an MET-style process involving:

```text id="2w09q8"
M — Map / Environment

E — Explore / Interaction

T — Transform / Co-Adaptation

I* — Intelligence / Emergence
```

The exact interpretation is conceptual rather than a fixed biological law.

The important idea is that intelligence interacts with and transforms the entire loop.

---

## Intelligence Placement

The figure highlights that intelligence can be distributed across:

```text id="91crdd"
Physical Structure
Sensor
Analog
Boolean
CCC
ANN
World Model
LLM
```

The engineering objective is therefore not simply:

```text id="4e1a46"
Maximize model capability
```

but:

```text id="mxmqfb"
Optimize intelligence placement
```

---

## Evolutionary Interpretation

Evolution does not optimize for abstract general intelligence alone.

It operates under constraints such as:

```text id="ndqljw"
Energy
Latency
Material
Complexity
Reliability
Environment
Survival
```

This creates pressure for specialized local intelligence.

---

## Engineering Interpretation

For a function \(F\), HSCI asks:

$$
P^*(F)
\coloneqq
\arg\min_{P}
J(F,P)
$$

where \(P\) is a candidate intelligence substrate.

A conceptual objective can include:

$$
J
\coloneqq
\alpha E
+
\beta L
+
\gamma C
+
\delta R
+
\epsilon W
+
\zeta A
$$

where:

* \(E\) = energy,
* \(L\) = latency,
* \(C\) = computation,
* \(R\) = reliability / risk,
* \(W\) = physical cost / weight,
* \(A\) = adaptability.

---

## Core Principle

> **Put the smallest sufficient intelligence at the earliest effective layer.**

# 6. Fig-003 — Resonance, Anchor, Trigger, Organizer

## File

`figures/Fig-003-Resonance-Anchor-Trigger-Organizer.png`

## Role

**Primitive Intelligence Functions Figure**

This figure isolates the small functional elements that connect physical response with structural intelligence.

It is particularly important for understanding HSCI-002.

---

## Four Functional Elements

### Resonance

A selective response to an environmental pattern.

```text id="k0u2xh"
Pattern
   ↓
Selective Coupling
   ↓
Strong Response
```

Resonance can contribute to salience.

It is not equivalent to pleasure.

---

### Anchor

An Anchor marks something as locally important.

```text id="rbzcqq"
THIS matters.
```

It provides a structural reference.

---

### Trigger

A Trigger changes what happens next.

```text id="ml1jgv"
ACTIVATE this path.
```

It can initiate:

* computation,
* control,
* routing,
* memory,
* or action.

---

### Organizer

A Structural Organizer arranges neighboring information relative to a salient center.

```text id="40pd85"
Anchor
  ↓
before / after
near / far
toward / away
same / different
```

This creates the foundation for local structural maps.

---

## Important Clarification

The figure should not be interpreted as:

```text id="gq2sc5"
Resonance = Intelligence
```

Rather:

```text id="5cnbwz"
Resonance
   ↓
Salience
   ↓
Structural Role
   ↓
Intelligence-Relevant Consequence
```

---

## Starmap Connection

The Anchor and Organizer concepts connect to:


Thus a salient event can organize structure before complete semantics exists.

# 12. How to Read the Six Figures as One Story

The complete visual argument is:

### Step 1 — Fig-000

Intelligence is distributed across hardware, software, and environmental interaction.

### Step 2 — Fig-001

Representation is not necessarily the first point at which intelligence emerges.

### Step 3 — Fig-002

Once intelligence can exist at multiple layers, placement becomes an optimization problem.

### Step 4 — Fig-003

Small primitives such as resonance, anchoring, triggering, and organization can create early structural intelligence.

### Step 5 — Fig-004

Stable intelligence can move between software and hardware through co-evolution and structural consolidation.

### Step 6 — Fig-005

A drone provides a concrete system in which all of these mechanisms can be combined and measured.

The visual chain is therefore:

```text id="38lzqw"

        ↓
Before Representation
        ↓

        ↓
Primitives
        ↓

        ↓
```
